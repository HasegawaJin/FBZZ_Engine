# Waterfall authoring

## Connected waterfalls

Add **Waterfall** to an object, then assign **Upstream** and **Downstream** in the Inspector. The source uses the last section of a Spline River by default. The receiver uses the first section of a Spline River. The endpoint switches reverse those choices. To land in a lake or rectangular water body, place the Waterfall object at the impact point with the Scene View position gizmo. The generator fits the impact band to the wet footprint. Status reports missing water references or an obstructed path. `Rock Attached` lets the curtain follow terrain along its descent. `Breakup Amount`, `Spray Amount`, and `Mist Amount` independently control the visible effects.

Terrain Flow Network uses the same cross-section and geometry builder for **Build lake-fed waterfalls**. Automatic placement requires a Spline Lake supported by a terrain basin and a connected drainage path from its outlet to the cliff. The preview reads proposed excavation samples and shows the two bank-to-bank sections. A rejected cliff candidate keeps a continuous river reach. Apply and Undo treat the terrain, rivers, and linked Waterfall objects as one transaction. The generated meshes and emitters are transient. The saved scene stores water references and effect settings.

The lip and impact bands use the Spline River snapshot's bank vertices. The connected water surfaces locally taper their world-space displacement to zero around those bands. The water query and render paths consume the same pin regions. Between the contact bands, the curtain has a bounded 3D cross-section, gravity-shaped travel, animated thickness, and increasing aeration and breakup. Three emitters spawn droplets at the lip, along the fall, and at the impact. Their velocity comes from the fall height and entry flow. Downstream foam uses the receiver's flow speed. A separate mist emitter advects with scene flow fields and integrates local density to the first opaque surface.

The curtain and baked liquid use directional shadows for direct sunlight and keep a diffuse sky contribution in shade. Advected pockets of aerated water modulate opacity and specular roughness. The lower curtain adds metre-scale breakup so that turbulence stays visible near the impact of tall falls. The receiver foam decays downstream in patches. When replacing an authored waterfall with a connected one, disable any older foam, spray, and mist actors that occupy the same region to avoid blending the effects twice.

The curtain and foam share a vertex buffer and two index ranges. Each view selects a range from projected size in `WaterfallSurfaces`, after the receiving surface is drawn. Only input changes regenerate the mesh. Particle count, emission rate, and screen coverage bound mist and droplets. Mist evaluates one, three, or five density samples by projected diameter, using a single directional shadow lookup for larger puffs. The effect can render with scene-wide Froxel Fog disabled. Measure `WaterfallSurfaces` and the particle pass separately when comparing quality levels. See [GPU Gems 3, High-Speed, Off-Screen Particles](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iv-image-effects/chapter-23-high-speed-screen-particles) for the fill-rate tradeoff.

## Baked 3D impact water

Select a connected **Waterfall** and choose **Bake 3D Fluid** in the Inspector. **Fluid Size** is the width of the simulation region in metres. Assign any rocks the water should hit in **Fluid Rocks**. Enabled Sphere, Box, and AABB colliders supply their collision shapes. The bake samples terrain in the region and uses the receiving water surface as the impact boundary. **Fluid Emission** is the number of particles per second, **Fluid Grid** is the surface resolution, and **Fluid Frames** controls the duration at 24 frames per second. The connected waterfall supplies the curtain, fine spray, foam, and mist.

The editor snapshots the inputs and runs CPU PBF on a worker. It validates the inputs again before publishing a GUID-referenced asset under `Assets/Generated/Waterfalls`. The assignment supports Undo and Redo. Save the scene to keep the reference. Changes to the connected water, sampled terrain, or rock colliders require a new bake.

Each frame is a checksummed chunk with quantized positions and normals and 16-bit indices. Visible near views request frames by frustum and projected size. Only the requested frame is decoded and the engine reuses two upload buffers. The local mesh fades near its region boundaries. The connected procedural waterfall continues to render at distance and when a clip cannot be loaded. Playback is visual and the receiving WaterComponent remains the gameplay water surface.

## Legacy mesh and VFX prefab

Waterfall curtains are ordinary rigid MeshRenderer objects with transparent project materials. They do not extend WaterComponent into a vertical fluid volume. Water below and above the curtain retains the height-field query and buoyancy contract from [water-foundation.md](water-foundation.md). Flowing texture coordinates are visual animation, not simulated water transport.

`Assets/Shaders/Water/Waterfall.hlsl` supplies downward streaming, foam breakup and a radial impact-foam mode. The standard material Inspector reflects the parameters in `Waterfall.mat` and `WaterfallFoam.mat`. Duplicate a material before adjusting an individual waterfall. `flowSpeed` is UV repeats per second; curtain UV v=0 is the lip and v=1 is the foot. `streamDensity` controls the number of horizontal streaks, `foamAmount` mixes foam, and `opacity` changes visual coverage. Geometry is rigid, with custom transparent coverage; the shader declares no standard PBR, GBuffer or ray BSDF contract.

`Assets/Samples/Waterfall/Waterfall.vfx` is a normal VFX prefab with a curtain, impact foam, water droplets and mist. Edit the hierarchy through the existing prefab editor. Keep the root at unit scale when physical particle velocity and gravity must remain in world units. Edit the curtain width/height and foam footprint on their child transforms, and move the spray and mist emitters to the foot. A water curtain alone does not provide collision or buoyancy. Use ordinary rock collision and an actual WaterComponent for the receiving pool when gameplay requires them.

The prefab uses `WaterfallVolume.obj`, containing nine closed stream sections with varying elliptical cross-sections. Its normalized mesh maps to a 48 m width and a 64 m fall at child scale `(48, 64, 64)`; the reusable sample uses smaller child dimensions. The centerline starts with horizontal velocity and bends downward under constant gravity. This is an authored visual trajectory, not a fluid simulation. Match the upper river endpoint to the lip, place impact effects at the centerline foot, and clear the terrain across the complete stream width and depth. Check the result from the side as well as from the front.

OBJ files use right-handed authoring coordinates. `ModelImporter.cpp` applies `MakeLeftHanded`, `FlipWindingOrder` and `FlipUVs`, so an export generated from engine-space geometry must negate position/normal Z, reverse triangle winding and write OBJ V as `1 - fallTime`. After import, V increases toward the foot and positive `flowSpeed` animates downward. Apply this conversion at export; do not compensate with a reversed prefab rotation. ParticleEmitter velocity is emitter-local even in world simulation space, while gravity remains world-space.

The prefab uses the existing ParticleEmitter budget, distance LOD and lifetime controls. Droplets use gravity and stretched billboards; mist uses low-opacity billboards. Both loop continuously with fixed seeds. No fluid solver, new Scene phase, engine-specific gameplay script, bespoke asset format or duplicated reflection contract is introduced. This follows [vfx-prefab.md](vfx-prefab.md), [particle-authoring.md](particle-authoring.md) and [shader-capabilities.md](shader-capabilities.md).

Validation must distinguish standalone shader compilation, structural asset validation and actual in-map rendering. A baseline captured for the first time records a reviewed image; it does not prove a regression comparison. The open-world placement is authored as additional objects in the existing scene, preserving existing instance IDs and terrain assets.
